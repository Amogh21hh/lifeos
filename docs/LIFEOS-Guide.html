Introductory guide · no prior knowledge needed

LIFEOS, explained from the beginning

LIFEOS is a personal “command centre”. It keeps one connected picture of your goals, projects, deadlines, people, documents, money and opportunities, and continuously works out what deserves your attention, what you’re overlooking and what your options are. It always shows its reasoning, and it never acts for you without asking.

01

What LIFEOS is

Think of a very organised chief of staff who has read every email, every document and every to-do list you’ve ever handed them, and who sits down with you each morning to say: “Here are the seven things that matter today, here’s why, and here’s what you may have missed.”

LIFEOS is software that plays that role. It combines several tools people usually keep separately:

  • a planner (goals, projects, tasks, deadlines),
  • a personal CRM (people, conversations, promises you made),
  • an opportunity tracker (jobs, freelance work, courses, events),
  • a decision workspace (“Should I buy this car?”),
  • a document reader (contracts, invoices, job descriptions),
  • a money dashboard (cash, spending, runway),
  • and a searchable memory of everything above.

The difference from a normal app is that LIFEOS doesn’t just store these things. It connects them and reasons over them, so it can notice, for example, that a contract you uploaded months ago has a notice deadline next week that isn’t on any to-do list.

02

The problem it solves

Most people’s lives are spread across dozens of places: inboxes, calendars, notes apps, spreadsheets, PDFs, bank apps and their own heads. Each tool knows one slice. None of them can answer the questions that actually matter:

  • Of everything competing for my attention, what has the most impact today?
  • What am I forgetting or neglecting?
  • Is my time going to the things I say are most important?
  • Which opportunities fit me, and what would make them more likely to work out?
  • If I make this choice, what am I trading off, and what don’t I know yet?

Answering these needs all the slices at once. LIFEOS brings them together in one place and does that cross-checking continuously.

How it works in one minute

Everything in LIFEOS flows through the same five stages:

1 · COLLECT
Your informationGoals, tasks, people, documents, transactions, messages, typed in or synced from connected services.
2 · CONNECT
A map of your worldEverything is linked: this project proves that skill, which this job needs, at this company, where this person works.
3 · REASON
Analysis enginesRank actions, detect blind spots, compare options, simulate “what if”, find opportunities.
4 · EXPLAIN
Insights with evidenceEvery conclusion shows the records behind it, its assumptions, its confidence and what would change it.
5 · YOU DECIDE
Approve or ignoreAnything that would change data or send something is proposed first and only runs if you approve it.

04

Five ideas to understand

Once these five ideas make sense, the rest of LIFEOS follows from them.

1. Everything is connected

LIFEOS stores your information as a knowledge graph: items (goals, projects, people, skills, jobs, documents) joined by labelled links (“evidences”, “requires”, “works at”, “introduced”, “supports”). That’s how it can follow a chain such as Python → credit-risk project → a job that needs Python → the recruiter at that company → a message you haven’t answered.

2. Every conclusion is an “insight” with its reasoning attached

LIFEOS never just tells you something. Every recommendation is an insight you can open to see exactly why. Here is a real one from the demo:

Reply to Sophie Laurent (Meridian Insurance)
Reason: Finance Talent Acquisition at Meridian Insurance contacted you 6 days ago and no response has been recorded.
Evidence
  • FACT Email from Sophie Laurent on 27 Sept — needs reply
  • FACT FP&A Graduate Programme: second-round interview in 2 days
Why it ranks here
Impact1.53
Urgency2.56
Confidence0.90
Effort1.21
Assumption · What would change this
  • ASSUMPTION No reply was sent from a channel LIFEOS can’t see.
  • → A reply already sent from another channel.

3. Every fact says where it came from

Each piece of evidence carries a coloured label, so you always know how much to trust it:

LabelMeansExample
FACTRecorded directly in your data or a document.“Applications close on 7 October” from a job description.
ESTIMATECalculated from your data. An approximation.“Runway ≈ 4.9 months”.
ASSUMPTIONSomething the analysis takes as given and states openly.“Success rate assumed ≈ 12% for a job application”.
USER INPUTSomething you told LIFEOS.“Minimum freelance rate £35/hour”.
AI INFERENCEA pattern LIFEOS spotted that you should check.“The Kaggle challenge doubles as progress on your credit project”.

4. You stay in control

LIFEOS can read your information freely, but it can never change, send, spend or delete anything on its own. It works in four steps: propose → explain → ask you → only then do it. Pending actions wait in an approval queue (the bell icon, top right).

5. Specialist “agents” do the work

When you type a request in the Command Center, LIFEOS routes it to one of ten specialists, such as the Decision agent or the Project agent. Each one can only use the tools it’s been given. You can see every agent run, every tool it used and every record it touched on the AI activity page.

05

Getting started

Run it on your computer

  1. Install Node.js (version 20.9 or newer) from nodejs.org. Nothing else is required: no database server, no Docker, no API key.
  2. Install the app’s dependencies, once, from the lifeos folder:
    npm install
  3. Start it:
    npm run dev
    The first start creates a small database inside the project folder and fills it with demo data. This takes about a second.
  4. Open http://localhost:3000 in a browser and click Enter the demo workspace.

Two ways in. The demo workspace is a pre-filled, fictional person so you can explore immediately (sign-in: demo@lifeos.app / lifeos-demo-2026). Create account gives you an empty workspace for your own information.

07

A morning with LIFEOS

The demo follows Amogh, a fictional MSc Finance graduate in London on a two-year Graduate visa. He wants a credit-analyst or FP&A job with an employer that can sponsor his visa. He also freelances, studies for the CFA Level I exam and is building a portfolio. His information is deliberately messy, like real life. Here’s what LIFEOS surfaces when he opens it:

  1. “You have 7 meaningful things competing for your attention.”

    The top action: confirm tomorrow’s Meridian interview, because the recruiter wrote 6 days ago and hasn’t had a reply. Then reply to Priya at Halcyon Bank (applications close in 2 days), then finish the interview case-study prep.

  2. A deadline hidden in a contract.

    His tenancy agreement says he must give two months’ notice before the lease ends. LIFEOS worked out the last day to give notice and flagged that it isn’t on any to-do list.

  3. Time going to the wrong place.

    “Your highest-priority goal (landing a sponsored role) received only 4% of your logged time this week; freelance work took 36%.”

  4. An opportunity hiding in plain sight.

    On the Radar, an amber dot: “Use your Credit-risk model as evidence for the Halcyon Bank application.” The project demonstrates exactly the Python and credit-analysis skills the job asks for, but it isn’t mentioned anywhere in the application, and it’s blocked by a 20-minute dataset download.

  5. A referral path.

    An old classmate, Tom, offered to refer him at Brightwater, but they haven’t spoken in 74 days, and Amogh owes his friend Ravi an introduction to Tom. LIFEOS suggests one message that does both.

  6. He asks: “Should I spend £500 on a modelling bootcamp?”

    LIFEOS builds the model. With his current priorities, “defer until after the CFA exam” leads. The result flips if cash matters much more than it currently weighs, and the open question that matters most is whether employers value the certificate. The choice stays his.

  7. He types “Draft a reply to Priya”.

    LIFEOS writes a reply based on her actual message and the deadline, then puts “Send email to Priya Shah” in the approval queue. Nothing is sent until he clicks Approve.

08

How the reasoning works

None of this needs an AI model. The core reasoning is ordinary, transparent arithmetic and rules applied to your data, which is why every number can be explained.

Ranking actions (“leverage”)

leverage = impact × urgency × confidence ÷ effort
  • Impact: how important the goal it serves is (priority 1–5), plus bonuses for how well an opportunity fits and how valuable it is.
  • Urgency: rises steeply as a deadline approaches and is highest when something is overdue.
  • Confidence: how complete the underlying information is.
  • Effort: longer tasks count against the score, but gently, so important deep work isn’t buried under quick chores.

Finding blind spots

Seventeen separate checks each look for one kind of problem, such as “an application older than 5 days with no follow-up” or “a strong relationship with no contact for longer than is normal for that tie”. They run independently, and an empty world produces no warnings at all, because nothing is ever invented. You can tune the thresholds (for example, after how many days a goal counts as neglected) in Settings.

Fit between you and an opportunity

LIFEOS compares the skills an opportunity needs with your skill levels. It adds credit when a project proves a skill and when you know someone there. It deducts when there’s a hard blocker, such as an employer that states it can’t sponsor visas when you need sponsorship.

Decisions

Each option gets a score from 0 to 10 on each criterion, with a pessimistic and an optimistic value. The criteria have weights reflecting what matters to you. LIFEOS then:

  • adds up the weighted scores for the pessimistic, normal and optimistic cases,
  • tests each weight from 0 to double its value to find where the leading option would change,
  • checks whether any single uncertain score could flip the result,
  • and pulls in lessons from your past decisions.

Search

Search combines four signals:

  • matching words,
  • matching meaning (so “income” also finds “salary”),
  • filters understood from your question (“overdue”, “documents”, “decided”),
  • and the knowledge graph (“connected to Priya”).

Each result lists which signals matched.

Simulations

Each “what if” is a small model built from your own history, such as your actual hours per project over the last weeks or your average monthly spending. Every assumption is shown as a slider and every output is labelled an estimate.

Safety, privacy and control

The AI can…The AI can never, without your approval…
Read and analyse your information, rank, search, simulate, draft text, propose actions.Create or change records, send a message, search the web, delete anything, spend money, or make commitments on your behalf.
  • Every action is logged. The AI activity page shows each tool call, its permission level and which records it touched.
  • Approve once, run once. An approval runs exactly the proposed action, one time.
  • No hidden profiling. Memories have a visible source, confidence and “last verified” date. AI-suggested memories need your approval, and you can edit, dispute or delete any of them.
  • Local-only mode. In Settings you can stop LIFEOS from ever sending data to an AI provider. Everything keeps working.
  • Your data is yours. Settings → Export downloads everything as one file.
  • Security basics are covered: hashed passwords, secure sessions, protection against cross-site attacks, rate limits, strict checks on uploaded files (they are never executed) and API keys kept on the server only.
10

Where AI is (and isn’t) used

LIFEOS works completely without an AI model. That mode is called the local reasoning engine, and it’s what the demo uses by default.

If you add an AI key (Claude from Anthropic, or any OpenAI-compatible service), the model helps with language tasks only:

  • understanding unusual requests in the Command Center,
  • reading messy documents more accurately (it must quote the exact text for every field, and LIFEOS checks the quote exists in the document),
  • drafting the options and criteria for a new decision,
  • and writing short summaries and message drafts using only facts LIFEOS gives it.

The ranking, blind-spot checks, decision maths, simulations and search are always done by LIFEOS’s own transparent logic.

If an AI call fails or is unavailable, LIFEOS quietly falls back to its own logic and marks the run as “partial” on the AI activity page. It never invents an answer.

12

What’s real and what’s simulated

  • Real: everything you type or upload, all the analysis, search, decisions, simulations, approvals, memory and the activity log.
  • Simulated: the connected services (email, calendar, banking, job boards, LinkedIn, GitHub, messaging). They use built-in sample data so you can see how syncing works. Connecting real accounts (Gmail, Google Calendar, Open Banking and so on) is the next stage on the roadmap; the app is designed so each one can be plugged in without changing anything else.
  • Fictional: the demo person, the companies and the people in the demo workspace.
  • Not yet included: background processing while the app is closed, notifications, two-factor sign-in, and reading text from scanned images without an AI key.

Estimates such as success rates, runway or “weeks to finish” are aids to thinking, not predictions. LIFEOS labels them as estimates or assumptions for exactly that reason.

13

Questions people ask

Does LIFEOS decide things for me?

No. It ranks, highlights and models. For decisions it shows which option leads under your stated priorities and how fragile that is, but you choose. Anything that would change data or contact someone waits for your approval.

Do I need an AI subscription or API key?

No. Everything works with the built-in local reasoning engine. An AI key only improves language tasks such as reading messy documents and understanding unusual requests.

Where is my data stored?

On your own computer, in a database inside the project folder (or in your own PostgreSQL server if you configure one). Uploaded files are stored alongside it. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you add an AI key and leave “local only” switched off.

What if LIFEOS gets something wrong?

Every insight shows its evidence, so you can see why it’s wrong. You can mark an insight “Not relevant”, correct a document field (your correction is kept even if the document is re-read), and edit, dispute or delete memories.

Why does it say “7 meaningful things”? Where does that number come from?

It’s the number of candidate actions whose leverage score passes a threshold. Open any of them to see the four factors behind its score.

How do I get the demo back to its original state?

Settings → Your data → Reset demo workspace. This only works on the demo account.

It feels slow. What can I do?

The first visit to each page is slower while the development server prepares it. LIFEOS also needs a reasonable amount of free memory: close heavy apps (for example WSL/Docker, many browser tabs) or run a production build with npm run build then npm start.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to phone screens, with the main sections in a bottom bar. Open the app’s address from your phone on the same network, or deploy it to a server.

Glossary

Agent
A specialist part of LIFEOS (e.g. the Decision agent) that handles one kind of request using a fixed set of tools.
Approval queue
The list of actions LIFEOS has proposed but not done. Found under the bell icon and on the AI activity page.
Blind spot
Something you may be overlooking, found by one of the “What am I missing?” checks and backed by records.
Confidence
How sure LIFEOS is about an insight (low / medium / high), based on how complete the information is.
Evidence
The specific records behind an insight, each labelled with where it came from.
Fit score
How well an opportunity matches you: skills, proof of skills, contacts and constraints such as visas.
Insight
Any conclusion LIFEOS shows you, always with its reasoning attached.
Knowledge graph
The web of links between your goals, projects, people, skills, opportunities and documents.
Leverage
The score used to rank actions: impact × urgency × confidence ÷ effort.
Local reasoning engine
LIFEOS’s built-in logic, which works with no AI model at all.
Potential opportunity
An opportunity LIFEOS inferred from connections in your data (the amber dots on the Radar).
Provenance
Where a fact came from: FACT, ESTIMATE, ASSUMPTION, USER INPUT or AI INFERENCE.
Runway
How many months your available cash would cover your essential costs.
Sensitivity
How much a decision’s outcome depends on a particular weight or score.
Smallest useful next action
The first step of a project plan: concrete, usually under 30 minutes, and chosen because it unblocks or advances the most.
Tool
A single capability an agent can use (search, create a task, send a message), each with a permission level: read, write, external or destructive.
